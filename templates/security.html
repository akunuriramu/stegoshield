Defense in Depth

1. Steganography vs. Encryption

Security professionals must distinguish between secrecy of existence and confidentiality of content:

Steganography

Hides the existence of communication. An adversary observing traffic only sees an innocent image file. However, steganography alone provides zero mathematical protection if the carrier algorithm is known.

Encryption

Protects the contents of the message. The existence of ciphertext is overt, but its contents cannot be deciphered without the cryptographic key.

The StegoShield Synergy: Even if a state-level adversary or automated scanner detects the StegoShield binary header via steganalysis, they are confronted with an unbreakable AES-256-GCM ciphertext.
Cryptographic Rigor

2. Cryptographic Specifications

Key Derivation: PBKDF2-HMAC-SHA256

Conforms to OWASP recommendations with 600,000 iterations. Every encryption generates a unique, cryptographically random 16-byte (128-bit) salt using os.urandom to prevent rainbow table attacks.

Authenticated Cipher: AES-256-GCM

Provides both confidentiality and message authenticity. A fresh 12-byte (96-bit) initialization vector (nonce) is generated per message. The 128-bit authentication tag guarantees that bit-flipping attacks on the stego carrier cannot produce forged plaintext.

Integrity Checksum: SHA-256

A 256-bit hash is stored within the binary header. Decoders verify the payload digest prior to parsing, catching accidental pixel damage or compression artifacts immediately.

Honest Engineering

3. Limitations and Attack Surface

StegoShield does not make misleading claims of being "unbreakable". Users must be mindful of real-world operational constraints:

1. Vulnerability to Lossy Compression:

If the stego image is uploaded to services that convert images to JPEG or apply lossy compression (such as WhatsApp, Discord, Twitter, or Instagram), all LSB bits will be scrambled and destroyed.

2. Spatial Transformations:

Cropping, rotating, scaling, or resizing the stego PNG alters pixel alignments, making the binary sequence unrecoverable.

3. Statistical Steganalysis:

If an image is heavily saturated with payload bits (over 50% capacity used), statistical tools (such as Chi-Square attacks or Sample Pair analysis) can detect non-natural LSB entropy distributions.

4. Password Entropy:

AES-256 cannot compensate for trivial passwords (such as "123456" or "password"). Always use a high-entropy passphrase with at least 14+ characters.
